What feels the same

  • A set table with last time’s numbers already filled in. Type over them and tick each set.
  • A rest timer that starts on its own, plus supersets, warm-up and drop sets, and RPE.
  • Personal bests, an estimated one-rep max for every lift, and charts over time.
  • A plate calculator and templates you can start in one tap.

What’s different

  • No account and no server. Nothing is uploaded, so there’s nothing to sign in to, and nothing to leak. Backups are files you keep yourself, and you can lock them with a passphrase.
  • More than lifting. Yoga poses and breathing, runs, rides and classes, and a weight trend against a target, all in one log.
  • Form cues and drawings for every built-in lift, one tap away mid-set.
  • Several people on one phone, each with their own history.

Where Strong is the better pick

If you want an Apple Watch app, or your workouts synced across a phone and a tablet, Strong does that and Uplift doesn’t. Uplift keeps your data on one phone on purpose. To move to a new phone, you save a backup file and open it there.

Bring your Strong history

You don’t have to start again. Export a CSV from Strong’s settings and import it in Uplift: every workout, set, warm-up and cardio session comes across, and each workout name becomes a template. Step-by-step guide.
